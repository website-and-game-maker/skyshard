# Skyshard 2: The Hollow Sky (sequel plan)

Status: planning only. Nothing here is built. Written 2026-10-31.

This is the plan for a sequel to Skyshard, to start only if the first game earns it (see [Greenlight signals](#greenlight-signals)). It assumes the fixes now in progress on Skyshard 1 land first: smoother turning, the respawn camera fix, the tutorial and the extra levels. The sequel inherits all of that.

## The pitch in one line

Pip can now pick things up, carry them, throw them and use them on the world, and the world reacts: water grows plants, fire burns vines, light scares the dark. Pip uses all of it to dive below the clouds and find where the darkness in the sky comes from.

## What makes it a sequel and not more levels

Skyshard 1 is about getting good at three verbs: jump, dash, pound. Everything in it is something you stand on or avoid. The sequel keeps those three exactly as they are (players who finished the first game should feel at home in five seconds) and adds the thing the first game has none of: **objects you can hold and use**. Levels stop being obstacle courses and become places you poke at, where the fun question is "what happens if I bring this over there?"

Rule for every new interaction: it has to create a new kind of problem to solve, not just a longer gap. If a tool only makes existing jumps easier, cut it.

## Pip's new abilities

### Grab: the core verb (from the first minute)

One new button: **Grab** (E / gamepad X / a touch button). It does whatever makes sense for what Pip is next to:

| Next to... | Grab does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A small object (crate, fruit, key, crystal, a lost baby spirit) | Pick it up and carry it over Pip's head. Grab again to place it, or dash while holding it to throw. | Carry a key across a level without dropping it off an edge. |
| A heavy object (boulder, cart, big block) | Hold to push or pull it. | Shove a boulder off a cliff to make a bridge below. |
| A lever, crank, valve or rope | Pull it, turn it (hold and move the stick), or ring it. | Crank a drawbridge down while a timer runs. |
| A character | Talk, give them what you are carrying, or trade. | Bring a lost spirit back to its family in the hub. |
| An enemy you stunned with a pound | Pick it up and throw it. | Throw a stunned puffcap into another enemy, or onto a switch. |

Carrying changes how Pip moves: a crate means no double jump, a boulder means no jumping at all, a balloon fruit makes you float. That alone makes plain platforming feel new.

### Tools: one per world

Tools go in a pouch. A second new button, **Use** (Q / gamepad Y / a touch button), uses the selected tool; switch tools with 1 to 4, the d-pad, or a swipe on the tool button. Touch screens still need only four buttons total: jump, dash, grab, use.

| Tool | What it does | The problems it creates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cloud jar** | Scoop up water (or cloud) and pour it out. Can hold one thing at a time. | Grow plants, put out fires, fill a basin to float a raft up, freeze a puddle into an ice platform with frost. |
| **Seed pouch** | Plant seeds in soft ground. Watered seeds grow into vine ladders, bounce flowers or bridge-leaves, and keep growing while you watch. | Build your own route up a cliff; grow a bridge, then burn it behind you so an enemy cannot follow. |
| **Shardline** | A tether. Fire it at a glowing anchor to swing, or at an object to yank it toward you. | Swing over voids, pull a key off a ledge you cannot reach, drag a platform into place from a distance. |
| **Lantern** | Carry a light. Lights fuses and torches, reveals hidden platforms, and gloomwisps will not come near it. | Escort the light through a dark level; set it down to hold back the dark while you work, and pick it back up before it goes out. |

**Glide** (hold jump in the air) and **wall kick** (jump next to a wall) come back as passive moves you earn in the first world. They need no button.

### The world reacts (this is the "cool things" part)

Every object has a few simple properties (wet, burning, frozen, lit, heavy, floats), and they react to each other by a small table of rules. Players will find combos we did not plan:

- Water + seed = plant. Fire + vine = ash and a gap. Frost + water = ice. Ice + fire = water again.
- Light + gloomwisp = it flees. Lantern + fuse = boom. Wet crate does not burn.
- Thrown objects knock enemies over, press switches and break cracked walls.
- Wind carries anything light: a dropped leaf, a balloon fruit, Pip while gliding.

Each world has hidden shards that only clever combos reach, and the hub has a "tricks found" board that counts them.

### The hub is something you build

Things you carry home (furniture from wrecks, plants, lost spirits) can be placed in Pip's house and around Lanternreach. Rescued spirits move in and open shops, side quests and music. It gives collecting a purpose without gating the story.

**Upgrades** (bought with shards, never required to finish): bigger cloud jar, faster Shardline, longer-lasting lantern, an extra heart.

**Cut on purpose:** combat weapons (swords, shooting). Skyshard is a movement and tinkering game. Enemies stay something you stomp, pound, dodge, throw things at, or outsmart with the world.

## Worlds

A hub plus five worlds. Each world has 3 main stages, 1 challenge stage (hard, optional, big shard reward) and a boss or set piece. That is about 20 stages, roughly 6 to 8 hours for a first run, more for 100%.

| World | Look and feel | New thing | Set piece |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hub: Lanternreach** | The beacon island from Skyshard 1, now a small town of sky spirits that grows as you bring things back (new houses, lights, music layers). | Placing things | Fills in over the whole game. |
| **1. Gale Steppes** | Golden grass islands, huge sky, wind you can see. | Grab and throw, glide, wall kick | Carry a giant fragile egg home across collapsing islands while a stormfront chases you. |
| **2. Brightwater Shoals** | A sea of cloud with half-sunk islands and crystal reefs. | Cloud jar | Flood and drain a sunken temple room by room to float a raft up through it. |
| **3. Bloomvale Orchard** | Overgrown terraces, giant flowers, a garden gone wild. | Seed pouch | Grow a beanstalk-like tower to the top of the world while a fire spreads up from below. |
| **4. Frostfall Canopy** | Icy giant trees, slippery momentum, snow that hides edges. | Shardline | Pull apart the frozen dam of a giant tree to free a river, then ride the flood down. |
| **5. The Underlight** | Below the cloud floor. Dark, glowing, upside-down islands. Gravity flips at marked seams. | Lantern | Carry the last light through the dark to the Gloam, using every tool. |

Skyshard 1's enemies return (mossback, puffcap, gloomwisp) with world variants, plus one new enemy per world that tests that world's idea: thieves that steal what you carry in the Steppes, sponges that drink your water in the Shoals, weeds that strangle your plants in the Orchard, ice-armored mossbacks you must yank the armor off in Frostfall, and shadows that snuff your lantern in the Underlight.

## Story

Skyshard 1 ends with Pip relighting the beacon. The sequel starts the next morning.

1. **The light reveals a hole.** The beacon's light reaches further than anyone expected, down through the clouds, and shows something below: a second, dark sky, the Underlight. The gloomwisps Pip fought all came from there.
2. **The Lanternreach spirits** (new characters: a nervous lamplighter, a cloth-weaver who makes the glide cloth, a gardener, a tinkerer) each know part of the old story and give Pip a tool.
3. **The Gloam.** Something below is pulling light out of the sky, which is why shards keep scattering. Every world Pip clears, the hole gets smaller and the Gloam gets louder.
4. **The twist.** The Gloam is not a monster. It was the first sky spirit, the one who built the beacon, and it fell below the clouds long ago. It has been pulling at the light because it is trying to climb home.
5. **The ending.** The final fight is a chase, then a choice: Pip uses every tool not to defeat the Gloam but to build it a way up: growing the vines, freezing the steps, carrying the light. The beacon now has two keepers. Post-credits: the true ending for 100% shards shows the second light turning on.

Tone stays light and wordless-friendly: short text bubbles, no voice acting, every story beat readable from what happens on screen. Cutscenes are skippable and under 30 seconds.

## Tech changes

The Skyshard 1 code is a good base: modules talk only through `main.js`, every movement number lives in `src/player/tuning.js`, and there are unit, playthrough and browser tests. The sequel needs these changes, roughly in this order.

1. **Abilities as plug-ins on the player.** `Player.js` today handles jump, dash and pound directly. Before adding carrying, glide, wall kick and four tools, split it into a small state machine where each ability is a module with `canStart`, `update` and `end`. Carrying is a state too, because it changes which other moves are allowed. Tuning numbers stay in one file per ability.
2. **An object system (the biggest new piece).** A new `src/objects/` module: every grabbable thing is an entity with a shape, weight and properties (wet, burning, frozen, lit, floats). It needs simple physics of its own: gravity, bouncing, landing on colliders, being pushed, being thrown. My strong opinion: write this small physics ourselves instead of adding a physics library. A library is 200+ KB, fights the hand-tuned player feel, and still makes stacked boxes jitter. We keep it simple instead: crates snap to a grid when stacked, nothing tips over by accident, and every object comes to rest quickly.
3. **The reaction table.** One data file of rules (`water + seed -> sprout`, `fire + vine -> ash`) checked when objects touch or when a tool hits them. Adding a combo means adding a row, not code. Each rule gets a unit test.
4. **Interaction prompts.** A small icon over whatever Grab would act on, so the player always knows what the button does right now. Picking the right target when three things are close (nearest in front of Pip wins, characters beat objects) is the part that makes this feel good or awful, and it gets its own tests.
5. **Levels as data, loaded per world.** Skyshard 1 has one hand-placed level in `src/level/layout.js`. The sequel stores each stage as a data file (islands, movers, objects, soil patches, anchors, switches, shards, enemies, characters) and loads each world as its own lazy chunk, so the first download stays under the current 600 KB gzipped budget and later worlds load while you play the hub.
6. **An in-browser level editor** behind a `?edit` URL flag: place islands, objects, anchors and shards, test a reaction on the spot, fly a free camera, export the stage file. 20 stages are not practical to build by typing coordinates.
7. **Save system.** Three save slots in `localStorage`, with a version number and migration so an update never wipes progress. Saves store tools, shards per stage, best times, upgrades, and where every placed object sits in the hub. Also export and import of a save as a text code, since school Chromebooks often clear storage.
8. **Camera upgrades.** Camera hint volumes (a stage can say "look this way here" or "pull back here"), a wider pull-back while gliding, swinging or aiming a throw, and the respawn fix from Skyshard 1 made into a rule: the camera never starts closer than its normal distance.
9. **Dialogue and cutscenes.** A tiny scripted sequence player (move camera, show bubble, wait, play sound) driven by data, not code.
10. **Adaptive music.** The procedural synth from Skyshard 1 gains layers that fade in with ability use and hub progress.
11. **Performance.** Instanced meshes for grass, crystals and props, a cap on how many loose objects can move at once (sleeping objects cost nothing), distance-based detail, and a per-world frame budget checked in CI with the existing automatic quality system as the safety net.
12. **Accessibility.** Assist mode (slower game speed, extra hearts, skip a stage after several deaths), full control remapping, colorblind-safe shard and hazard colors, reduced motion and camera shake toggles.
13. **Tests.** Every ability and tool gets unit tests for its edge cases (throw a crate into a wall, carry a lantern into water, grow a vine through a moving platform). Every stage gets a scripted playthrough test like `src/level/playthrough.test.js` proving it can be finished with only the tools you have at that point, and that no object can end up somewhere that soft-locks the stage (every key and crate respawns at its start if it falls off the world). That test is what stops us shipping an impossible stage.

**Same repo or new one?** Same repo, as a `skyshard2/` entry point that shares the engine folders. Splitting into two repos would mean fixing every bug twice.

## Greenlight signals

Right now nothing measures whether Skyshard is a hit. Step one is to start measuring, and then decide on real numbers instead of a feeling.

**Measure first (small task, needs Saahir's OK):** an anonymous play counter on the same Val Town setup Unblock Games already uses. It records only events like "started", "finished tutorial", "finished level N", "beat the game" and "came back on another day", with no names, no IPs stored, nothing per person. Plus the shared feedback button already planned across Skyshard and Unblock Games.

**Greenlight if, within 60 days of the full Skyshard 1 release, at least three of these are true:**

| Signal | Bar | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plays | 1,000 game starts | Enough people found it. |
| Tutorial finish rate | 60% of people who start it finish it | The controls and camera fixes worked. If this is low, fix Skyshard 1 before building a sequel. |
| Full game finish rate | 15% of starters beat the game | People cared enough to see the end. For browser games this is high. |
| Comeback rate | 25% of players return on another day | It is a game people choose, not one they tried once. |
| Asked for more | 10 or more feedback messages asking for more levels, a sequel or new moves | Direct demand. |
| Saahir still wants it | Yes | The most important one. A sequel nobody is excited to build will not be good. |

**If it misses:** do not build the sequel. Spend the effort on Skyshard 1 instead (whichever signal failed points at what to fix), or ship a free "Skyshard: Gale Steppes" expansion with just grab, throw and glide, which costs a fraction and tests whether new abilities bring people back.

## How it gets built

Built the same way as Skyshard 1: parallel workstreams, each in its own thread, with one integration thread.

| Phase | What | Size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Measure | Play counter and feedback button on Skyshard 1 | Small, one agent |
| 1. Foundations | Ability state machine, object system and reaction table, data-driven levels, save system, level editor | Big, 3 agents in parallel |
| 2. Vertical slice | Hub + Gale Steppes fully finished with grab, throw, glide, story intro and music. This is the second go/no-go: if the slice is not more fun than Skyshard 1, stop or rethink. | Big, 3 agents |
| 3. Production | Worlds 2 to 5, one agent per world, each owning its tool, enemy and stages | Big, 4 agents in parallel |
| 4. Story and polish | Cutscenes, ending, hub building, upgrades, accessibility, performance pass | Medium, 2 agents |
| 5. Test and ship | Full playthrough tests, cross-device checks, deploy next to Skyshard 1 on GitHub Pages and Unblock Games | Medium, one agent |

My recommendation: do phase 0 now, because it is cheap and useful even with no sequel. Do not start phase 1 until the signals say yes, and treat the phase 2 slice as a hard checkpoint. The riskiest part of this plan is the object system: if carrying and throwing feel clumsy, the whole game does. So the slice must nail Grab first, and should include a small test room with water and seeds even though those worlds come later, to prove the reaction rules are fun before building four worlds on them.

## Open questions for later

- Should the sequel have a co-op mode (second player controls a second spirit)? Carrying and throwing to each other would be great fun, but it doubles the testing. Decide after the slice.
- A sandbox island in the hub where every tool and object is available to mess around with, unlocked after the ending. Cheap once the object system exists. Probably yes.
- Echo (a ghost that replays your last few seconds) was in the first draft of this plan. It is cut for now in favor of objects, and could come back as a late-game tool if the slice goes well.
